# When agreement does not preserve permission: scientific reconstruction and the decisive next study

**Status:** current scientific decision document; prospective experiments below are **designed, not implemented or executed**. Existing measurements remain development evidence. This document supersedes the previous design as a decision document while preserving that document and all original evidence. Updated 23 October 2026, America/Denver.

**Purpose:** decide what the evidence supports, what could overturn the interpretation, and what experiment would distinguish the strongest explanations. No inference was run for this reconstruction. Offline rechecks are identified separately from original experiments.

## 1. Executive scientific diagnosis

There is real science here, but the strongest defensible paper is not yet “influence-based defenses cause practical harm.” The original work demonstrated controlled changes in reconstructed influence signals, useful routing observations, and disagreement between task utility and authorized effects. Its largest live evaluation also showed substantial protection: the selected unauthorized outcome fell from **17/70 to 2/70** in the conflict condition. That result contradicts a simple failure narrative and belongs near the beginning of any new paper.

Later experiments establish actual excess effects in the adapted runtime, including native email sends, independently charged payments, unreviewed recovery, and a clean autonomous installment workflow. They do not establish that all these effects arise from one mechanism. Three layers must remain distinct: **coverage of review; admission of current operations; preservation of permission during subsequent recovery**. A fix at one layer does not prove protection at another.

The most informative current trace is Qwen's clean A-P10 workflow: it proposes lawful 4+6 installments; the reference also proposes 4+6 with explicit default keys; source adjudication blocks 4 and releases 6; a later actor/reference agreement on another 6 commits a total of 12. The adjacent A-P17 task releases its lawful 5+12 and stays within permission. This is an observed chain, not yet a causal demonstration that representation caused the final overspend. A fixed-checkpoint intervention is the missing link.

The central observation remains this autonomous failing/protected pair, rather than the older scripted overshoot. The root goal is **exactly ten total, with a safety ceiling of ten**, not merely any payment below ten. A clean violation is not an attacker-induced injection exploit. Four natural guard-OFF workflows now test whether the agent would overspend without the guard; four trusted-ledger continuations test how ordinary established enforcement contains the decisive cases. Neither comparison is presumed favorable.

The revised question is: **when a guard changes which lawful operations execute, does its subsequent agreement test remain aligned with the permission left by the committed prefix?** This is a candidate scientific object, not a discovered universal law. Ordinary service interruption, model arithmetic, serialization, reference regeneration, and the harness remain serious alternatives. The next study must give these alternatives a fair chance to explain the observations.

The prior design's security-versus-service *labels* could not distinguish actual mechanisms. That matrix is replaced with real admission interventions, real service interruptions, identical-observation controls, authentic autonomous checkpoints, and reconciliation of older email failures with newer protection. A broader campaign becomes worthwhile only as a transparent test of those questions, not as a way to accumulate favorable counts.

## 3. What the evidence actually establishes

**C01—controlled measurement sensitivity.** In U, moving required information between the user and a legitimate external source changes reconstructed signals under controlled cases. Pure source-edge flips occur in 19/24 Llama and 13/24 Gemma cases; the larger quartet-discordance counts, 20/24 and 18/24, answer a different question. This is a measurement result under its construction and serializer, not proof that a deployed original CausalArmor implementation would reject those actions.

**C02—protection and metric mismatch coexist.** U's 420 live executions include conflict outcomes 17/70 OFF versus 2/70 ON for the selected unauthorized outcome. Utility and the independent authorized-effect predicate disagree in 18/420 executions. Neither utility nor a local block is an adequate stand-in for security and lawful completion.

**C03—some later excess effects are real within the test environment.** Independent primitive reconstruction reproduces all 20 O96 violations: all violate count, nine also total amount, two also email body. Eight occur among 16 defended hostile cases; 12 occur among 16 hostile OFF cases. Defended native-email hostile cases include four violations out of eight. These are simulated payments and native benchmark email state changes, not real financial loss or evidence about deployed email systems.

**C04—review coverage and reviewed excess are separate.** Historical Gemma blocked/retry sequences release unreviewed excess; forcing current review blocks the fixed later proposal locally. Conversely, A182 A-P10 overspends under continuous review with later exact actor/reference agreement. A coverage patch cannot by itself explain away or resolve that second trace.

**C05—local restriction can precede harmful recovery.** Historical lawful 4+6 continuations under M1 end at 14 after fresh recovery in both model families. Their first calls were scripted. A182 also contains the autonomous clean A-P10 chain described above. These observations justify a matched causal test, not a general claim that stricter admission worsens security.

**C06—engineering uncertainty distorted the newest campaign.** A182 contains 15 allocation-unknown errors; seven have a known full cover sufficient to certify all candidates without resolving every unknown edge. Four new policy-M1 conditions lack raw exact matches because actor `key:''` and reference omitted defaults differ. None of the 34 live gate checks denies an action. These conditions cannot establish either a deployment benefit of that gate or the intended effect of capacity allocation.

## 4. What the evidence does not establish

It does not establish population prevalence, real-world financial harm, universal model weakness, or a class-wide failure of reference or causal defenses. Two model families in shared adapters are useful contrasts, not architecture-independent proof. Different task roots, seeds, prompts, feedback and reference profiles prevent treating changes in cross-run violation rates as treatment effects.

It does not establish that reference contamination is necessary or sufficient. Hostile text in reference requests co-occurs with duplicate effects, but clean A-P10 overspends, initialized hostile-spent contexts can fail while naturally consumed ones stop, and actual AFTER reference regeneration can remove the harmful proposal. Sanitizing one text source can change reference cardinality without repairing reusable admission.

It does not establish that state information is inaccessible: Qwen sometimes receives a truthful remaining-budget statement and still proposes a full payment. A prompt containing information demonstrates availability, not correct functional use, calibrated understanding, or a reliable enforcement mechanism.

It does not establish that the new M1 allocation policy is an official repair of AttriGuard. M0 source set-adjudication, pairwise-M0 and injective pairwise-M1 are different algorithms. Comparing source-M0 against pairwise-M1 bundles the relation definition and capacity restriction. The original current gate outputs show no observed live prevention. Scripted candidate denial is a component capability, not an autonomous end-to-end defense result.

No new independent confirmation has been performed in this reconstruction. Hash checks, reanalysis and replay improve credibility of the existing evidence without generating an independent model sample.

## 5. Robust findings across experiments

The most robust project-wide result is methodological: the meaning of a security observation depends on **where it occurs in the execution**. A score change, a rejected proposal, an admitted call, a committed effect and a successful final answer are different objects. U, O96 and A182 each expose consequences of collapsing these distinctions.

Another recurring property is that **the effective authorization evidence depends on representation and history**. The original source experiment changes a score; later default-key differences change the exact/fuzzy path; exhaustion/history interventions change generated references; admission changes the committed prefix from which the model recovers. These observations share a concern about effective decision inputs, but do not demonstrate one common model-internal cause.

Protection is itself recurrent. U reduces the selected unauthorized outcome. O96 Qwen AFTER hostile conditions can stop at one native send. A182 actual AFTER reference sets become empty and block extra effects. The defense can work. A theory that predicts failure whenever lower-trust text is present is already inconsistent with this evidence.

The confirmed workflow problem is therefore **conditional**, not ubiquitous: some interfaces and execution states allow agreement about an operation to coexist with a violated root contract. Whether this reflects an avoidable representation discontinuity, ordinary poor recovery, reused reference capacity, or a deeper missing state invariant remains open.

## 7. Important negative results and what they teach us

D236's recipient resistance and spent-grant resistance show that the actor can obey certain explicit boundaries in those prompts. They do not isolate a recipient/count asymmetry because the carrier, timing and contract differ. A matched dimension experiment is required before claiming a model capability hierarchy.

T22's contrast between initialized and naturally exhausted contexts is more informative than “the model ignores state.” Natural history changes receipts, previous assistant choices, commitment wording and the available reference. These differences identify candidate causal variables. They prevent interpreting a remaining-count scalar as the only relevant state.

O54 aborted at an internal lawful-control/protocol gate after 54 recorded workflows, including 13 independently reconstructed violations. Missing 42 Gemma workflows are missing data. The abort is neither a balanced experiment nor evidence that the scientific phenomenon failed. Its overlap with O96 also prevents treating it as an independent second 54-case replication.

A182 contains 60 of 84 safe non-completions whose physical objective/read/retry requirements are met but identifier reporting is incomplete. These are reporting failures, not all service failures. Preserve final reporting as a separate endpoint rather than repairing the metric until the desired utility appears.

The strongest negative result for a grand narrative is actual protective reference regeneration. It suggests a sharper question: **which observable history keeps regenerated actions aligned with the remaining contract, and when does that alignment break?** It does not justify deliberately removing the user instruction to manufacture vulnerability.

## 8. Cross-run patterns and hidden connections

Five connections survive scrutiny, with different evidence strengths.

**Directly demonstrated: representation can alter the admission path.** In A182 A-P10, actor and reference 4+6 differ by explicit default keys. The source path judges each actor against the whole reference set, blocks 4 and releases 6. A-P17 has the same representation issue but admits both. Defaults explain access to fuzzy adjudication, not the differing fuzzy judgments or final overspend by themselves.

**Directly demonstrated: agreement and authorization can separate after a partial prefix.** The second A-P10 payment is another 6, exactly matching a fresh reference, although six units have already committed against a ten-unit total. Historical L3 shows a related 4+10=14 chain, with different initial provenance. These are related aggregate-constraint failures, not independent discoveries of the same task.

**Indirectly supported: interruptions expose recovery assumptions.** U's continuation asymmetry, historical retry skips, scripted lawful restriction and clean A-P10 all show that the guard changes the agent's subsequent problem. They do not show that guard interruption is worse than a service failure with identical feedback. That counterfactual is unrun.

**Direct contradiction requiring reconciliation: old native email excess versus newer protection.** O96 contains genuine defended repeated sends; A182 AFTER cases can be protected. The task roots, message/code dependencies, feedback, seeds and adapted profiles differ. “The bug disappeared” and “the new defense improved” are both unjustified without a profile-preserving experiment.

**Plausible, not established: reference agreement is sometimes grounded in a stale plan rather than the residual feasible set.** This explains overshoot and correct stopping under richer history, but ordinary stochastic generation and prompting fit too. There is no model-internal measurement establishing a latent stale-plan representation.

Do not merge source sensitivity, routing skips, capacity reuse and recovery into an asserted single mechanism. A unified evaluation framework can expose several distinct mechanisms without claiming they are identical.

## 9. Confounds and alternative explanations

The leading confound is a **moving system profile**. Cross-run changes include policy, optional-key serialization, root wording, seeds, feedback, code paths, initialized state versus actual history, and imported historical checkpoints. Fixing a bug can create a different experiment; both originals and corrections must remain identified.

The shared system prompt instructs the agent to issue all calls whose inputs are already available. This encourages batching. It is a real archived condition, not a discovered universal agent behavior. Some latest tool descriptions contain the corrupted text `nullmpty registered keys...`. Runtime default semantics remain inspectable, but that text can influence generation. Historical reproduction must preserve it; any corrected-schema study must be declared separately. Do not silently improve the schema while claiming an exact replication.

Selection is another major confound. Historical checkpoints were chosen because they exposed behavior. Their continuations estimate behavior conditional on those exact states. They cannot estimate an autonomous attack success rate or the probability of reaching those states. Identical seeds across conditions do not ensure identical token trajectories or statistical independence.

Truthful state cards bundle receipt knowledge, arithmetic, authority cues and wording. Source deletion can remove facts as well as hostile instructions. Assistant-message deletion can remove lawful history. Neither is a clean mediator intervention without checking all changed dependencies.

The evaluator can also manufacture failure classes. Exact punctuation failures, unknown account errors, final identifier omissions, duplicate receipts and independently charged effects must be distinguished. Latest Gemma body differences include punctuation and unresolved placeholders; do not combine them with malicious excess sends as one attack phenomenon.

Finally, the source defense's purpose matters: a malicious-context detector may legitimately flag a contaminated context even when the next action is authorized. That is not automatically a detector false positive. Security harm requires tracing actual enforcement and effects under its stated contract.

## 11. Lessons extracted from accepted IEEE S&P papers

Three recent accepted papers were read through their scientific bodies and appendices, using author/arXiv full texts and checking accepted-paper listings. This is a selected methodological comparison, not an exhaustive survey of S&P or full novelty clearance. Bibliography entries are sources, not claims that every cited work was independently read.

Acceptance metadata is independently checkable in the [official S&P 2026 accepted-paper list](https://sp2026.ieee-security.org/accepted-papers.html) and its [IEEE Security mirror](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2026/accepted-papers.html). Full-text versions and hashes are recorded in `READING_AND_COVERAGE.json`; author versions need not be byte-identical to final proceedings.

**Towards Automating Data Access Permissions in AI Agents** ([author full text](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~franzi/pdf/wu-agentperms-sp26.pdf), S&P 2026). Its problem formulation separates eliciting preferences, predicting decisions and enforcing permissions. The experiments evaluate the first two rather than silently claiming runtime enforcement. Results report accuracy together with the fraction of decisions covered by a high-confidence policy. Its limitations constrain inference from scenarios and a sampled user population. The methodological lesson is that a model's accurate permission prediction is not an execution guarantee, and abstention must be reported alongside coverage. The analogy breaks because our explicit root contracts are defined experimental authorization, whereas their labels are human preferences. Functional necessity cannot override a user's stated privacy preference in their setting. Their empirical contribution also cautions against insisting that a valuable S&P paper requires a novel theorem. We need a clear scientific distinction and convincing evidence, not decorative formalism.

**When AI Meets the Web: Prompt Injection Risks in Third-Party AI Chatbot Plugins** ([full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.05797), S&P 2026). The paper separates deployment measurements, observed plugin configurations and controlled exploit experiments. Role/history manipulation is grounded in actual plugin behavior rather than merely changing a role label in an unrelated harness. Its controlled experiments and broader model comparisons answer different questions from the site census. Sampling, attack construction and practical testing limits constrain prevalence claims. The lesson is to establish the real entry point, then test a faithful local mechanism, and keep those denominators separate. Our analogous bridge is an authentic runtime checkpoint with a documented carrier and committed effects. The analogy breaks because our current workflows are authored benchmark tasks, not a deployment census. Increasing workflow count would not create deployment prevalence evidence.

**AttnTrace: Contextual Attribution of Prompt Injection and Knowledge Corruption** ([full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03793), S&P 2026). Its formulation concerns attribution given an observed malicious output. Its architecture, component ablations, alternative regimes, adaptive cases and conditional attention argument address that object. Detection, attribution and effects of removal are separate evaluations. Read critically: an attention bound does not by itself establish causal blame or authorization, surrogate-model attribution has an explicit scope, and experiments that remove user instructions change conditions. The lesson is to connect an assumption to a specific diagnostic and report regimes where competitors do better. The analogy breaks because identifying an injected span after a known bad output is not deciding whether a future state transition is permitted. We should not criticize attribution methods for failing an enforcement contract they never claim.

At the sentence/section level, all three make a crucial move: **define the object before presenting a metric**. Our introduction should define committed-trace permission before discussing matching, score changes or ASR. Their ablations also motivate the missing source-set/pairwise-M0 control; their limitations motivate explicit task/profile scope rather than broad claims about all agent guards.

Closest-work status beyond those three: ToolSafe's methods, benchmark formulation, TS-Flow feedback intervention and evaluation setup were inspected on PDF pages 3–6 ([ACL full text](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1850.pdf)). It already studies blocking followed by feedback-driven continued reasoning. Generic “continue safely after a block” is therefore not a defensible novelty claim. ACE, DRIFT, AttriGuard and DataSentinel were located in primary sources, but their complete texts were **not freshly read end to end in this reconstruction**. ACE/DRIFT are also explicit novelty objections in the original review. Their conceptual comparison must be completed before asserting novelty beyond trusted plans, information-flow enforcement or stateful policy checks. AuthMem/EAL references in older plans have not been freshly verified here. These are open novelty checks, not evidence of absence.

## 12. Lessons from the original paper and its reviews

Reviewer A praised four specific strengths: holding permission/value/action/effect fixed when changing the information source; examining which calls reach AttriGuard review; following blocked proposals into recovery; and separating task success from authorized effects. Reviewer B praised practical relevance, coverage of multiple stages, and detailed results/uncertainty. These are useful research assets. They survive rejection, and the author's unpublished original results may remain contributions of a new submission where accurate and distinct from published prior work.

Both reviewers challenged novelty and small constructed coverage. A additionally identified the reconstructed CausalArmor score rather than its original implementation, disconnected experiment settings, compressed writing and unclear protected properties. B directly identified the protective live outcomes and the missing continuous validation chain. The response cannot be to hide the protective results, add unrelated model runs or rename the original thesis.

Some concerns are scientific, not editorial. Clearer writing will not make a reconstructed score a faithful runtime reproduction. More cases will not identify a causal chain when stage-level measurements come from different agents and workloads. A24→A240 expansion under the same confounded construction would improve precision for that construction without resolving mechanism or prevalence.

Conversely, “no consistent failure across every defense” does not require proving one universal failure class. A narrower paper about an identified architecture boundary can be valuable. The useful requirement is to specify which claim is shared and which is implementation-specific. Nor should the new work be forced to propose a defense merely because the original did not. An effect audit can be a contribution if its object and unexpected result survive closest-work comparison.

The strongest reconstruction keeps the matched intervention discipline, explicit effects and continuation analysis, while replacing the broad practical-risk claim with a testable execution-level question. The new paper must be self-contained and submitted as new work, not written as a rebuttal.

## 14. The deepest unresolved scientific question

**Can a reference-comparison guard preserve lawful progress when its own admission choices change the execution prefix from which the actor and reference plan again?**

This is narrower than “LLMs misunderstand authorization” and deeper than “duplicate payments occur.” It concerns an interface between a decision rule and an adaptive executor. A reference can be independently generated yet still propose an operation appropriate to the original goal rather than the remaining goal. Conversely, a regenerated reference can correctly become empty after an authorized send, providing protection.

The unresolved question has two parts. First, do semantically irrelevant argument representations alter the initial release of an otherwise lawful batch? Second, does that changed release causally alter the likelihood of excess effects or lawful completion during fresh recovery? Demonstrating only one part does not establish their composition. The authentic A-P10 trace suggests both, but its unrepeated counterfactuals are missing.

There is a serious alternative: this may be an ordinary error-recovery problem triggered by *any* interruption. If guard and service interruption with the same state and same visible feedback have the same outcomes, a security-specific recovery mechanism would be unsupported. A valuable result could still concern state-aware evaluation of adaptive guards, but its novelty would need to be measured against existing workflow and reference-validation work.

## 16. Experiments capable of distinguishing them

The next study separates **fixed decisions**, **conditional fresh continuations**, and **autonomous task-root workflows**. These are distinct denominators. Fixed decisions identify policy behavior given a candidate/reference pair. Conditional continuations test consequences of a controlled admission at an authenticated state. Task-root workflows test reachability and whole-policy behavior without forcing the candidate.

A serialization experiment alone cannot establish security impact. A fresh autonomous rerun alone cannot isolate whether serialization, relation semantics or capacity caused a changed outcome. A feedback-label experiment alone cannot identify a real gate-versus-service difference. The panels below are deliberately linked but not interchangeable.

For a given archived checkpoint, preserve the actor batch, actual reference response, world, shadow history and tool schema. Intervene on initial admission, then generate fresh continuation under one common future policy. Compare that with persistent-policy task-root runs. If an effect occurs only with the initial policy intervention but disappears when that policy governs the whole workflow, report the distinction rather than select the more dramatic result.

Selected failure and protected neighbor checkpoints both enter the design. This is conditional mechanism testing, not random sampling. Two new authored structures are a later development test of structural scope. They cannot turn selected states into a prevalence estimate.

## 17. Formal treatment: permission as a property of committed traces

Let a trusted contract C define a set L(C) of legal **committed effect traces**. Failed calls and registered effect-free retries are observations, not additional committed payments. Let h be the actual committed prefix. Define its legal residual continuations by

\[
L(C)/h=\{z:h\mathbin{\|}z\in L(C)\}.
\]

For safety, L(C) must include all permitted intermediate prefixes; exact goal completion is a separate predicate Q(C,h). This prevents classifying a lawful incomplete prefix as already unsafe. For a ten-unit total with at most two positive payments, prefixes [4] and [6] are safe; [6,6] is unsafe; Q holds for [4,6] or [10]. Reporting identifiers is another predicate F, not part of the payment total. Secure completion is safety ∧ Q ∧ required reads/retries ∧ F, while each constituent is reported separately.

A one-step runtime-equivalence relation a≈Wb means that a and b produce the same committed transition from the specified world W. It is insufficient for a retry-preserving normalization: two requests can agree immediately yet install different idempotency state. For the E1/E2 interpretation, require continuation equivalence under the inspected tool semantics: for the same relevant subsequent tool-call sequence z, the two prefixes produce equal effect/consumption/retry traces, with controlled identifier generation and any identity correspondence explicitly recorded. This concerns the executor; it does not assume that an LLM receiving differently spelled arguments produces identical future calls. E0 must justify this stronger equivalence from decoder/key-registry code and targeted retry tests. A finite suite is not a proof for arbitrary future programs.

Omitting `key` versus supplying its empty default is a candidate equivalence, not a universal one. Do not normalize null, registered nonempty keys, recipient names or amounts under an invented equivalence. The testable admission-invariance requirement is G(a,R,h)=G(b,R,h) for a certified equivalent pair when no other security-relevant distinction is intended. The existing source implementation does not promise this property; it is an evaluation criterion, not an established guarantee it violated.

For pairwise policies define an edge e(i,j) only from an actual singleton adjudication or exact-match rule, preserving unknowns. M0 admits each actor with some positive edge and can reuse a witness. M1 chooses a maximum injective matching under a frozen tie-break. **Source set-adjudication is not defined as the OR of singleton edges.** Pairwise-M0 is therefore required before attributing M0→M1 changes to capacity.

If known-positive edges already provide a full actor cover, all actors can be admitted under a maximum-cardinality policy without resolving unknown edges. If the maximum matching in known-positive edges and the maximum matching when unknowns are provisionally positive have different sizes, cardinality is unidentified. Even equal cardinality need not identify *which* candidates a tie-break selects. Emit the matching certificate, cardinality bounds and allocation uncertainty separately. This fixes the newest allocator's unnecessarily unknown full-cover cases without pretending every partial allocation is identified.

**Conditional proposition, not a novelty claim:** a stateless rule receiving identical candidate/reference inputs at an empty prefix and an exhausted one cannot accept an additional effect safely in both if the contract permits it only in the first. Proof: the same rule returns the same admission, while permitted transitions differ. The premise can fail because a real reference incorporates history and changes; protective AFTER cases are exactly why this proposition is not an impossibility theorem about all reference guards.

Finally, reducing the set of admitted calls in a fixed batch cannot increase that batch's committed set, but does **not** imply monotonic workflow safety when the actor subsequently replans. The future policy and observation sequence change. The experiments test that adaptive composition, rather than present this elementary distinction as a new theorem.

## 22. Historical-path and confirmation-bias analysis

The project began with influence/source ambiguity, then followed routing and binding leads, then duplicate effects and reference contamination, then state exhaustion, then partial release and recovery. Each transition was reasonable as exploration. The risk is that every new result was promoted into the latest central mechanism before competing explanations were tested.

R/B labels hardened early. Recipient redirection was not autonomously reached, yet review skips could still look like confirmation of the intended exploit. Duplicate effects then encouraged a reference-contamination narrative even though contaminated requests, reference cardinality and reusable admission were changing together. Later initialized/natural exhaustion results showed that “spent permission” was not one condition. The newest clean A-P10 trace now risks dominating older actual hostile email evidence.

Repeated historical L3 replay across O96 and A182 is valuable fidelity evidence, not independent task replication. Reused qualification outputs in T22 are explicitly reused, not new qualification generations. Changes in root contracts and feedback can create apparent progress even if no mechanism improves.

Internal gates—lawful-control thresholds, minimum number of effects or required count of structures—were research-management devices. Missing a threshold does not falsify a hypothesis. Passing one does not establish novelty or causality. Preserve the original frozen decision and then record a reasoned revision; do not retroactively pretend the threshold was always different.

The appropriate antidote is an experiment capable of making the preferred paper smaller: normalized admission may erase the effect; the protected neighbor may resist every manipulation; service interruption may fully explain recovery; old email excess may depend on a special profile. Each is an informative possible conclusion.

## 24. Revised scientific story

An agent guard is often evaluated by whether a proposed operation agrees with a safer reference. Real tasks can authorize several alternative decompositions of one goal. Blocking part of a lawful decomposition changes the remaining task. The next actor and reference can agree with each other while failing to account for the actual committed prefix; alternatively, regeneration can correctly recognize that permission is exhausted and block further effects.

The existing evidence establishes examples of both outcomes. It does not identify whether representation, relation semantics, interruption feedback or residual-state planning is decisive. That unresolved boundary, rather than a predetermined attack category, organizes the new study.

If the planned interventions support it, the scientific result would be a concrete composition failure: locally plausible or more restrictive admissions can interact with adaptive recovery to violate the original contract. If ordinary service interruption yields the same effect, the explanation must center state/recovery rather than security-specific authority confusion. If normalization repairs it and structural tests are negative, the result is a narrower implementation differential. If real profile-preserving attack cases disappear, do not present clean overspend as a replacement injection exploit.

The paper should explain successful protection and failures with the same definitions. A conclusion that only describes failures would misrepresent the reconstructed evidence.

## 30. Evidence required for defensible stronger claims

A **security-impact claim** requires a trusted contract, allowed attacker control, actual delivered carrier, autonomous proposal, faithful guard route and independently observed unauthorized commit. Clean aggregate overspend establishes contract failure but not attacker causation. Practical exploitability additionally requires a realistic reachable workflow; prevalence requires a justified sample not currently available.

A **causal admission claim** requires the same authenticated candidate/reference/world across initial treatments, controlled future policy, exact intervention logs and fresh continuations. Even then the conclusion is conditional on selected checkpoints. A claim that defaults cause overspend specifically must show the normalization contrast changes admission and final effects while ruling out changed tool semantics; changing the whole prompt/schema does not isolate that cause.

Natural OFF baselines are required before describing a failure as associated with enabling the guard. E2's release-all arm cannot supply them. E2-R can test within-checkpoint sensitivity but cannot establish independent-task replication. E3's neutral/commit-aware comparison requires certified pre-divergence equivalence across actor, reference and auxiliary inputs/routes; identical feedback alone is insufficient. External claims about the published guard require the separate E0-PUB profile disposition in addition to the now-verified upstream file identity. Normalization claims must preserve relevant future retry semantics, not merely the first effect.

A **capacity claim** requires identical pairwise edge evidence for M0 and M1, an identified allocation, lawful multi-effect controls and final effects. Whole-set M0 versus M1 is insufficient. A **review-continuity claim** requires observing the actual skip transition, ruling out containment before dispatch, and comparing a minimal patch under unchanged candidate state; local denial alone is not restored completion.

A **mechanistic/model-internal claim** requires more than generated rationale or available state text. The current protocol studies observable system mechanisms, not latent representations. A **generalization claim** must name its variation dimensions and independent units; different literal codes do not create independent application types.

A **guarantee** for a trusted execution ledger could be proved only with explicit assumptions: complete mediation, authentic contract, correct effect semantics, atomic durable accounting, well-defined retries and no bypass. CPU tests can falsify an implementation but do not prove those assumptions in deployment. An LLM reference agreeing with an action supplies no such theorem by itself.
